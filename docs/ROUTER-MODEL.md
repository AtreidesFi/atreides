# Router model

The router plans an order across eligible venues using the books, balances and cost inputs available at quote time. The fields below appear in the order quote or in the detail returned by `GET /v1/route/{routeId}`. The detail includes the initial plan and any subsequent plans. Estimates in those plans describe the information available before execution.

For deployment evidence and public preview assumptions, see the [agent guide](../AGENTS.md).

## Eligibility and sizing

- **Accounts and instruments.** The planner considers accounts in the caller’s allowed scope with an active connection and a key that can submit the requested order. It matches equivalent instruments in the requested group and requires opt-in for mixed exposure classes. Direct routing requires a venue that carries the client order identifier into fill evidence. The plan shows the chosen `accountId`, `venue`, `instrumentId`, `cls` and `keyPlace` on each leg. `candidates[].dropped` names exclusions for candidates that reached the planner.
- **Trading state.** The planner checks the venue and instrument state, trading session, underlying session where relevant, and the switch governing openings. A protective close can still be eligible when openings are paused. `candidates[].dropped` records the reason a candidate failed these checks.
- **Book freshness and bounds.** The planner uses recent bids or asks and drops a marketable candidate with an unusable book. It checks the order’s explicit price or slippage bound, the venue price band and, when applicable, the price of the user’s own resting order on the opposite side. A permitted book midpoint can anchor the band when the mark is within the fallback window. The plan shows `arrivalPrice`, `worstPrice`, each leg’s `limitPrice`, and candidate `depthBase`, `bandCentre` and `markAgeMs`.
- **Capacity and native sizing.** Opening size is constrained by available collateral, the configured buffer and applicable leverage. Sells or reductions that draw on a holding use the attributable position or balance. The planner converts the request to each instrument’s native units, rounds to its lot and price grid, and checks minimums. Protective reductions use the lot grid without the planner’s opening minimums. The plan shows `capacityBase`, `leverageAssumed`, `size`, `baseSize`, `coveredSize`, `uncoveredSize` and `dust`.
- **Request budget and recent rejects.** Known low request headroom or a high share of recent congestion-related rejects can exclude an account from an opening plan. Hyperliquid uses its stored address budget, adjusted for actions sent since the reading. Other venues can supply headroom when available. `candidates[].executionHealth` shows the sample and `candidates[].dropped` identifies the exclusion.

## Pricing and selection

- **Depth and order instructions.** The allocator walks executable levels and ranks candidates by covered size and all-in price. It then favours fewer legs and breaks ties deterministically. Resting plans use a single venue. The planner respects the leg limit and checks that every venue in a split supports immediate execution. A plan with attached take-profit or stop-loss instructions requires one leg. The plan shows `execution`, `coveredSize`, `totals.allInPrice` and each leg’s `tif` and `expectedPrice`.
- **Quote conversion.** A configured absolute USD reference converts the venue’s native quote prices into a common unit. A stale configured reference excludes an opening candidate. Listed legacy stablecoins can use a one-dollar conversion while the reader is unconfigured. USD1 requires a USD reading and mixed stablecoin books are checked against the depeg bound. The leg’s `quoteReference` shows the applied rate and observation time when a reference is supplied.
- **Venue fees.** The model uses observed account maker and taker rates when present and marks a public table fallback where allowed. A Lighter taker plan requires evidence that the account can take liquidity and a compatible key. The plan leg shows `venueRate`, `venueFeeUsd` and `venueFeeEvidence`, including its source and observation time. The report shows recorded fees under `venueFeesUsd` when available.
- **Our fees.** The model applies the fee rule and applicable approval cap, including the effect of maker versus taker instructions. It can add a capped share of a qualifying split’s saving when the selected legs can encode that fee and a saving remains. The combined fee is shown in `ourRate`, `ourFeeUsd` and `totals.ourFeeUsd`. The filled route shows `ourFeesUsd` and `refundUsd` when recorded.
- **Fixed costs.** A gas-charging venue with an estimate contributes one modeled network cost per leg. `gasUsd` is the planned cost and `gasCostKnown` identifies whether the estimate was available where required.
- **Funding.** When a holding horizon is supplied for a perpetual opening, the forecast counts scheduled future payments through the end of the horizon. It uses the current rate where available for the first payment and the seven-day mean for later payments. A known calendar with no payment in the horizon contributes zero. An incomplete calendar or rate forecast remains unknown. The plan exposes `nextFundingAt`, `fundingSettlements`, `fundingUsd` and `fundingKnown`. The report exposes the expected funding and the forecast difference from the benchmark when supported by the evidence.
- **Latency and drift.** When timing and price observations are available, the model scales one-second price volatility by the square root of the time at risk, using measured median send-to-answer time and a separately supplied venue delay. Expected adverse drift affects the opening order’s modeled price and can tighten its leg limit within the parent’s bound. The leg’s `latency` shows its inputs, drift in basis points and drift in USD.
- **Cost of splitting.** For a discretionary split, the planner compares the split with a comparable single-venue order. The saving must exceed the estimated incremental cost to exit using the opposite-side books plus the configured margin for fragmentation across venues. Without enough evidence for this comparison the planner can choose the single venue. A split that is needed for coverage or used for a protective close can take a separate decision path. `splitDecision` gives the result and reason and can include `exitCostBps`, `fragmentationBps` and `thresholdBps`.

## Fill and outcome evidence

- **Execution savings.** The report compares the fills and known paid execution fees against the stored single-venue benchmark for the filled size. Final execution savings and the expected funding difference share the same benchmark notional. `report.savingParts` shows the benchmark, final or pending execution savings, the funding forecast and any exclusions.
- **Route completion.** A subsequent wave keeps the original worst price. Completion applies the final wave’s lot dust. The route shows `state`, `endReason`, `replans`, `filledSize` and the leg results. A terminal shortfall can show `shortfall` with the chosen disposition and remaining size. The status `unwind_requested` records a protective unwind request.
- **Markouts.** Route detail shows fill-notional-weighted price movement for horizons of 1, 5 and 30 seconds. For each fill the calculation uses the first valid midpoint or mark from the horizon through one second later. A positive markout favours the side that traded. `legs[].markouts` shows the signed basis points, sample coverage and reference type. An incomplete sample leaves the leg-wide basis-point value unknown.
